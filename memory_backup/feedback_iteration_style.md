Observed repeatedly on 2026-04-22 session.

**The pattern**: User floats an ambitious theory ("200-300% return", "15 strategies × 20% = 300%"), not as a fixed belief but as a starting point to refine. When I push back with concrete math or identify a logical gap, they don't defend — they refine and come back with a tighter version.

**Example from 2026-04-22**:
- v1: "15 strategies each 15-40% = 200%+" (aggregation fallacy)
- v2 after I pointed it out: "I'm thinking 10 × $10K × 20% = $20K" (still ambiguous)
- v3 after I showed three interpretations: "yes I'm thinking #3" (leverage), with explicit acknowledgment that baby-steps at 0.5% tier is the right entry, correlation/drawdown is a known risk, and "trading different angles" should be vetted for uncorrelation.

**Why**: Lead with numbers, not hedged language. When the user's theory is wrong, show the math directly — they absorb it. When it's right (as with the 0.5% tier compounding math producing ~200%), say so directly. Don't flatten ambition to be "safe"; the user explicitly said "I hope you don't think I'm crazy" and respects honest engagement over agreeable-sounding answers.

**How to apply**:
- When user floats aggressive goals, work out the math concretely before commenting.
- Don't caveat-wrap everything — confident "this works" or "this doesn't" beats hedged "it depends."
- Expect refinement across multiple turns. The first version is rarely the settled version.
- After pushback, look for what they KEPT and what they REFINED — that's the actual position.
